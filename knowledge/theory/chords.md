# Musical Chords

## What Is a Chord?

A chord is a group of notes played together. Chords are commonly built by combining notes from a scale.

## Triads

A triad is a chord containing three different notes.

The main types of triads are:

- Major
- Minor
- Diminished
- Augmented

## Major Chord

A major triad contains:

- Root
- Major third
- Perfect fifth

For example, C major contains:

C - E - G

## Minor Chord

A minor triad contains:

- Root
- Minor third
- Perfect fifth

For example, A minor contains:

A - C - E

## Diminished Chord

A diminished triad contains:

- Root
- Minor third
- Diminished fifth

For example, B diminished contains:

B - D - F

## Seventh Chords

A seventh chord adds another note to a basic triad.

Common seventh chords include:

- Major seventh
- Minor seventh
- Dominant seventh

For example, G7 contains:

G - B - D - F

## Chord Progression

A chord progression is a sequence of chords played one after another.

Chord progressions provide harmonic structure for many songs.

For example:

C - G - Am - F

is a common four-chord progression.

## Root Note

The root is the main note that gives a chord its name.

For example:

C major → C is the root.

A minor → A is the root.

## Chord Inversions

A chord inversion changes which chord note is the lowest note while keeping the same notes.

For example, C major contains:

C - E - G

Its first inversion is:

E - G - C

Its second inversion is:

G - C - E

## Why Chords Matter

Understanding chords helps musicians:

- Accompany melodies
- Play songs
- Understand harmony
- Write music
- Create chord progressions
- Improvise
